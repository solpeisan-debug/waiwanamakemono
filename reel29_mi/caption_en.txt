ST elevation — where in the heart? Can you localize an MI on a 12-lead?
---------------------------------------------------------
Hi, I'm Den, a nurse from Japan 🇯🇵

The leads with ST elevation give you a good idea of WHERE the infarct is and WHICH artery is involved.
Here are 6 patterns worth knowing.

🔍 How to read it
ST rises in contiguous leads → that's the infarct site
Leads on the opposite side show ST depression (reciprocal change)

1. Anteroseptal → V1–V4 / LAD
2. Extensive anterior → V1–V6, I, aVL / proximal LAD
3. Lateral → I, aVL, V5, V6 / often LCx (or an LAD diagonal)
4. Inferior → II, III, aVF (reciprocal in aVL) / usually RCA (sometimes LCx)
5. Right ventricular → inferior changes + ST elevation in V1 and V4R / proximal RCA
6. Posterior → ST depression with tall R waves in V1–V3; ST elevation in V7–V9 on the back / RCA or LCx

🚨 ST elevation → 12-lead ECG and notify the provider immediately (also chest pain, diaphoresis, dyspnea, hypotension, altered mental status)
💓 Anteroseptal / extensive anterior → watch for VF, heart failure and shock
🐢 Inferior → bradycardia and AV block are common (up to ~20%). Get right-sided leads (V4R)
🩸 RV infarction → prone to hypotension. Nitrates can cause a sudden BP drop, so avoid them in RV infarction (follow the provider's orders)
🔙 Posterior → no standard lead faces the posterior wall directly. It shows up as ST depression (+ tall R, upright T) in V1–V3, so add V7–V9 to confirm

📍 Place chest electrodes by the ribs, not the nipples
V1 4th ICS, right sternal border / V2 4th ICS, left sternal border / V3 midway between V2 and V4 / V4 5th ICS, left midclavicular line / V5 and V6 same level as V4, left anterior and mid-axillary lines
V7–V9 on the back at the same level as V6 (left posterior axillary line, left scapular line, left paraspinal); V4R = right 5th ICS, right midclavicular line (mirror of V4)
Electrode colors in this video: AHA

※ST elevation in V4R fades quickly (gone within ~10 hours in about half of cases). Record it early in inferior MI
※Posterior MI usually comes with inferior or lateral MI (isolated in only 5–10%)
※Lateral ST elevation can be subtle. If you see ST depression only in III/aVF, look for elevation in I and aVL
※Lead–territory–artery correlations are a guide only (coronary anatomy varies)
